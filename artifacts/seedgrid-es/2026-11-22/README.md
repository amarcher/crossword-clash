# Spanish crossword set for review — Sunday 22 November 2026

> **The 15×15 is not a candidate.** It is kept as an example of the open problem: it fails both Spanish fill rules (three same-root groups, 15 conjugated verbs against a budget of 6) and no rebuild has been tried. Review the mini, midi and Plus; read the large only to see what the rules are for.

Four draft crosswords in Spanish for one day: Santa Cecilia, patron of musicians, kept as the Día de la Música / Día del Músico in much of the Spanish-speaking world. Nothing here is published. They were built by machine fill from a private word list, with clues written by hand by a non-native writer, so they need a fluent reader before anyone plays them.

Each folder has `puzzle.ipuz` (the playable file) and `review.md` (the same content as the section below, plus how it was built).

## Grid conventions used
- Fully crossed, American numbering, no entry shorter than three letters, rotational symmetry.
- **Ñ is its own letter** and crosses as Ñ in both directions (it is never N).
- Accents and the diéresis are dropped in the grid (CANCIÓN is entered CANCION); clues keep normal spelling.
- CH, LL and RR take two squares.
- The four puzzles share no entry of four or more letters. Three-letter words may repeat (LEO, ASA, OLA do).

## What to check
1. **Is every answer a word you would expect a general solver anywhere to know?** Flag anything regional, archaic, or that you had to look up. The "Score" column is the word list's guess at commonness (62 very common, 50 the floor, "theme" a hand-placed entry); low scores are where to look first.
2. **Is every clue correct, natural and neutral Spanish?** Mark regionalisms (the clues lean Latin American in places: auto, básquet, repollo) and anything a native writer would phrase differently.
3. **Does each clue match its answer's part of speech, number, gender and tense?**
4. **Do the question-mark clues in the 15×15 work as wordplay?**
5. **Tone:** nothing should be crude, sad or gross.
6. Each puzzle's **"Not sure of"** list is where the builder already has doubts. Please rule on each.

## Two rules added after the first pass (13 Nov)
Each puzzle's check list now ends its grid section with them, and each review names its conjugated verbs.
- **No two entries of one root.** Forms of one word may not share a grid: OIR / OIA / OIDO, OSO / OSA. When the link only exists if a noun is read as a verb (ARO beside ARAR), it is listed as a note for you to judge by the clues, not failed.
- **A budget of conjugated verbs:** 1 in a mini, 3 in a midi, 4 in the Plus, 6 in a 15×15. Only forms that can be nothing but a conjugated verb count (PUSO, IRÉ); infinitives, participles, gerunds and words that are also nouns (CANTO) do not. The numbers are a first proposal: say if they are too strict or too loose.

The lemma data behind both is inferred from spelling, not from a dictionary, so it misses word families (AMORES and AMAS both come from amar and pass) and can be wrong about rare forms.

## Known weaknesses across the set
- The 15×15 breaks both rules: OIR / OIA / OIDO, OSO / OSA, SER / ERASE, and 15 conjugated verbs (APODERO, CAVAMOS, ACUDO, PROHIBA, OIA, ORE, REI, EMITE, ARRASO, ANDE, ERASE, VAS, ARDO, ALEGRO, NADE).
- Proper names appear as fill (ANA, ADAN, IRENE).

### Not sure of
- **OIR / OIA / OIDO**: Three forms of the same verb in one grid (35D, 10D, 39A). OIDO is clued as the noun (the sense of hearing), but an editor would reject this. Every floor-50 fill of this skeleton had OIDO and OIR; OIA is avoidable with more curation.

- **ALEGRO**: Clued as the tempo (RAE accepts 'alegro' for Italian 'allegro'; many musicians write 'allegro'). The other reading is 'alegró' (cheered up).
- **Conjugations**: Too many for one puzzle: REI (reí), ORE (ore), ANDE, NADE, CEDO, ACUDO, ARDO, VAS, OIA, EMITE, PROHIBA, CAVAMOS, APODERO (apoderó), ARRASO (arrasó), ERASE (érase). REI, ORE and ARDO are the least natural.
- **APODERO**: Rare verb (apoderar: to grant power of attorney). Weakest long entry.
- **FACES**: Plural of 'faz'; literary.
- **RECATO / SESO / IBIS / CRIOLLA**: Real words, lower frequency. CRIOLLA is clued through Peruvian 'música criolla'.
- **ARRASO**: 'Arrasar' also means to devastate; clued in its cheerful sense (to sweep the awards).
- **ENARMONIA**: The pun is thin: 'en armonía' is nearly literal. It is the only 9-letter music idiom that fitted the centre; A UNÍSONO (ALUNISONO) was the alternative.
- **DARELDODEPECHO**: Idiom is common in Spain; understood but less used in parts of Latin America.
- **Blocks**: 42 black squares (18.7%) is on the high side for a 15×15; the usual ceiling is about 38.

## How the puzzles were made
- Engine: the Ingrid wasm. The first pass used the committed build, which took only ASCII letters, so Ñ travelled as the digit 1. On 13 Nov the wrapper was changed to take Ñ as a letter and the wasm rebuilt; the mini and the Plus corner were refilled with it, with Ñ in the list as itself.
- `scripts/seedgrid/es/fill.mts` ranks fills by the two Spanish rules before list score, and with `strict` drops any fill that breaks one.
- `scripts/seedgrid/es/finish.mts` writes each puzzle and runs the checks printed at the end of each `review.md`. `scripts/seedgrid/es/readme.py` rebuilds this file's puzzle sections from them.
- Puzzle sources: `scripts/seedgrid/es/puzzles/*.json` (`mini-canto.json` is the first mini, kept for reference).
