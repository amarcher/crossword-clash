No login is required to play. The app creates an anonymous session automatically; signing in and the subscription are optional.

Core path: on the menu tap Play under Daily Mini. Tap a cell and type with the crossword keyboard. With Autocheck on (the default) wrong letters are rejected; with it off (the switch under the scoreboard in the clue sheet) any letter can be typed and the grid checked when full. Close and reopen the app to see saved progress.

Multiplayer (two devices, or one device plus a browser): Race friends > Start a room, pick a puzzle, enter a name and create the room. On a second device open Join with a code (or crosswordclash.com > Join) and enter the six-character code. The host can start once two players are in. Host as TV shows the shared board on a big screen.

Accounts (optional): Sign in with Apple or Google from "Sign in" at the top of the menu; an account keeps the username, solve history and streaks on every device. Any Apple ID or Google account works; no test account is needed. Deletion: Your account > Delete account, or crosswordclash.com/support.

Crossword Clash Plus (auto-renewable, plus_monthly $3.99 / plus_annual $24.99, each with a 1-month free intro offer for new subscribers; the paywall shows "1 month free, then" the price): from the Plus row on the menu or Your account > About Plus. The paywall lists the benefits (full daily archive, the construction studio with AI clue drafting, progress synced across devices, more AI narrator time on the TV view), both plans with prices, Restore purchases, and the Terms of Use and Privacy Policy. The app asks for sign-in before buying so the subscription follows the account. StoreKit only; nothing links to a web checkout.

Community puzzles and the studio (user-generated content): Plus subscribers build a crossword in the studio and publish it under their username; anyone can play published puzzles from "Community puzzles". AI clue drafting only drafts text the author asked for; nothing reaches other players until the author publishes. Every published puzzle has Report (Copied / Offensive / Nonsense / Other) and "Hide this author"; reports are emailed to the developer, three reporters in a week hold a puzzle automatically, and the developer can remove puzzles and block authors. Publishing requires accepting the community rules in the Terms of Use. Display names and usernames are filtered for objectionable words and can be reported through the Support page.

Friends (optional, accounts with a username): add friends by username or from rooms you shared; the other side accepts, declines or blocks. Friends can be invited into a room or sent a daily "beat my time" link; invitations are fixed messages with no free text and expire. A friend inviting you, and the players of a room you join, can see whether you already solved that puzzle (never your time).

Notifications (optional): the app asks for permission once, a moment after the menu appears on first open; declining leaves everything off and the account page points at Settings. Kinds: a daily reminder (off by default), room invitations and friend requests (on by default), with quiet hours. No notification carries text written by another player.

Optional import: Import from NYT opens nytimes.com in a separate in-app browser; the person signs in on NYT's own page. On Import the app first asks NYT whether this visitor has an active subscription that includes the crossword and refuses otherwise; only then does it read the open puzzle. The app bundles no NYT content or credentials, does not see the sign-in, and keeps solo imports on the device; hosting a room sends a copy to the other players, as the app explains first. Not affiliated with The New York Times. Review can be completed with the built-in puzzles.

No ads, no third-party analytics SDKs, no tracking. First-party play statistics (puzzle progress, time spent, per-clue timings) go to the developer's own backend, as the privacy policy describes.
