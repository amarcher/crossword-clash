# Spanish dailies need borrowed letters, built lists

Crossword Clash can publish its dailies in Spanish without inventing conventions, but it will have to build its own scored word list, and the 15×15 is the part nobody has shown to work. The letter rules are settled enough to adopt as they stand: **Ñ is its own letter, accents are dropped, and CH, LL and RR take two squares each**. A fully crossed "crucigrama americano" already exists in Spanish as a named format with the same rules as in English, and daily minis run at El País, El Debate and Costa Rica's La Nación. No scored Spanish fill list was found, free or paid. A commercially clean one can be assembled from public-domain lemma and conjugation lists, the tri-licensed RLA-ES spelling dictionary, CC0 Wikidata and Wikimedia pageviews, and CC BY Google Books Ngrams. The RAE dictionary, the FISE Scrabble lexicon and lists scraped from either must stay out. The market has no bylined Spanish daily with real-time races, friends leaderboards or a standalone subscription, though that conclusion is "not found", not "proven absent". Three things are unverified and matter most: whether a Spanish 15×15 fills at acceptable quality without two-letter entries, whether solvers raised on other grid styles want one, and whether anyone pays for Spanish puzzles outside a news bundle or print.

## Spanish tradition tolerates what American grids forbid

The traditional Spanish crucigrama is a looser form than the American grid. Spanish Wikipedia describes a numbered grid with "horizontales" and "verticales" and a sanctioned escape device: an answer that does not fit may be entered backwards, flagged "al revés" ([Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucigrama)). The same article lists the family of variants: the autodefinido (clues inside the grid), the crucigrama blanco, the silábico (one syllable per square) and the "con personaje" puzzle built around a photo ([Wikipedia ES](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucigrama)). The autodefinido is mainstream in Spain: one autodefinido app holds a **4.7 rating from about 2,500 ratings** on the Spanish App Store ([App Store ES](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/id1627932335)).

Explicit grid rules are almost undocumented. Newspapers publish puzzles, not style guides, and no Spanish-language source read states norms for size, black-square share, symmetry or minimum entry length. The concrete evidence is thin:

- One Mambrino puzzle for El País is described by its uploader as 12 × 13 ([Scribd](https://es.scribd.com/document/729494935/304)).
- One Spanish solver reports that El País grids have rotational symmetry, and that symmetry is likelier in letter-per-square puzzles ([Straight Dope](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/crossword-puzzles-in-other-languages/778514?page=2)).
- One current 15×15 from an independent site, Alejandro Cernuda's "Serpiente de Esparto" with 72 clues, shows what fill is tolerated ([acernuda.com](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)).

The Cernuda sample contains chemical symbols ("Símbolo químico: Tántalo"), ISO country codes, initials of authors, "Letra repetida", "Nombre de letra", a reversed initialism ("Invertido - Unidad de cuidados intensivos") and masked partial words such as "(***IMADO)" ([acernuda.com](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)). It is one site's software-assisted output, so it proves tolerance, not that leading setters use such fill at the same rate. The neighbouring French and Italian traditions explicitly allow two-letter words and need no symmetry ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)). Whether El País or La Vanguardia grids are fully crossed, and how they number clues, was not verified against a primary puzzle.

Letter handling is the firmest ground:

- **Ñ and accents:** accent marks are ignored in crosswords "except the tilde in Spanish", and N and Ñ are distinct letters ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)).
- **Digraphs:** CH and LL fill two squares ([Wikipedia EN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)). Mexican school guidance says the same, adds RR, and explains that accents are dropped because a crossing could put an accented vowel where the other word has none ([SEP](https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/coleccion/19242/)).
- **Ñ in software:** the Fortuny crossword app's version history records a fix for puzzles containing "ñ", which confirms it is entered as a letter ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1016442690)).

No source contradicts these for current practice. The treatment of ü is assumed to follow the other diacritics; no source states it. The claim that CH and LL once shared a square rests on an uncited Wikipedia sentence and is historical at most.

Clue register splits in two. The mass-market style is the dictionary definition, including the "Dicho de…" formula and country labels such as "Bol." for a Bolivian regionalism ([acernuda.com](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)). The prestige newspaper style is a short, sly "author's definition". Published examples are Mambrino's "lo que tiembla al llegar el postre" for FLAN, Tarkus's "lugar donde se hacen castillos" for AIRE and Olissip's "una casa de tortolitos" for NIDO ([elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)). This is oblique definition, not British cryptic, so it transfers to a fully crossed grid.

Solver testimony is sparse:

- A long-time El País reader finds Tarkus's dailies "harto difíciles" and preferred Mambrino ([Diario del Valle](http://diariodelvalledelaorotava.blogspot.com/2019/07/los-crucigramas-de-tarkus.html)).
- A reader of the Catalan daily ARA complained in 2026 that double-entendre clues were sexist ([ARA](https://es.ara.cat/defensor-del-lector/crucigramas-sexo-sensibilidad_129_5718923.html)).
- No thread complaining about two-letter fill or crosswordese was found. That is an argument from silence.

Themes mean something different. The attested Spanish forms are subject-based: magazines organised by sport, geography or the arts ([La Nación](https://lanacion.com.ar/opinion/crucigramas-mucho-mas-pasatiempo-nid2205687)), La Nación's "Cruci temático" product ([La Nación Juegos](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/juegos/cruci-tematico/)) and the pictured-personality puzzle. No evidence was found of long theme entries tied by a wordplay gimmick as a Spanish convention.

## Fully crossed Spanish exists mostly at 5×5

The American format has a Spanish name and a stated rule set. PasatiemposWeb sells a "Crucigrama Americano" "adaptados al público hispanohablante" in three difficulty levels. It describes rotational symmetry, far fewer black squares, every letter belonging to an across and a down word, and grids "habitualmente de 15x15 o incluso 21x21"; it says many have a central theme ([PasatiemposWeb](https://www.pasatiemposweb.com/crucigrama_americano.php)). The grids themselves were not inspected, and the page does not mention a minimum word length.

Other publishers of this kind of puzzle:

- **El País** announced "crucigramas mini" in July 2021 ([El País on X](https://x.com/el_pais/status/1413482349698994176)). Its grid rules could not be confirmed.
- **El Debate** runs a dated daily "Crucigrama mini" through 2026 with nine clues ([El Debate](https://www.eldebate.com/juegos/mini-crucigrama/2026-07-07/)). Nine clues implies at least one black square or a grid smaller than an open 5×5; that is arithmetic, not observation.
- **Crosshare** has a Spanish interface and Spanish-tagged minis ([Crosshare](https://crosshare.org/crosswords/GW3SO9COAM5ICKNkim9L/jueves-28-de-abril-de-2022-menu-espanol)). The constructor "elro" is described as publishing mostly American-style crosswords in Spanish ([Crosshare](https://crosshare.org/elromancin)); the profile was read only through a search snippet.
- **A bilingual Crosshare mini** enters its Spanish answers without accent marks ([Crosshare](https://crosshare.org/crosswords/3V66quRRU85HOsUnB4Rn/cruci-cross)).
- **The Washington Post** hosts Spanish crossword pages; their grid style is unverified ([Washington Post](https://games.washingtonpost.com/es/juegos/crucigramas-faciles)).

No Spanish-language crossword from the New York Times was found. Its Spanish news edition closed in 2019 ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2019/09/the-new-york-times-shutters-nyt-en-espanol-after-three-years-it-did-not-prove-financially-successful/)).

The largest gap in the research is that no constructor has written about filling a fully crossed Spanish grid. No source gives block counts, word counts, word-length statistics or a view on conjugations as fill. No Spanish 15×15 with wordplay themers was confirmed. The fully crossed Spanish output that could be found is overwhelmingly mini-sized. That fits 15×15 fill being hard, and it fits equally well with market habit; the evidence cannot separate the two. Giving up two-letter entries, reversals and unchecked squares removes all three traditional escape valves at once, so the mini and midi are low-risk and the themed large is an experiment.

## A clean scored list must be assembled in-house

No scored Spanish list in the `WORD;SCORE` style was found. The scored constructor lists are English ([Crossword Nexus](https://github.com/Crossword-Nexus/collaborative-word-list), [XWord Info](https://www.xwordinfo.com/Wordlist)). Crossword Compiler offers Spanish and Spanish-Wikipedia lists with undocumented scoring and terms ([Crossword Compiler](https://www.crossword-compiler.com/wordlists.html)). GNOME Crosswords' maintainers reached the same conclusion: the Hunspell Spanish dictionaries lack priority scores and need affix expansion ([GNOME Crosswords #39](https://gitlab.gnome.org/jrb/crosswords/-/issues/39)).

The sources sort into three groups by what their licences allow in a shipped commercial file.

Three caveats apply to this table. The RAE notices were read only as search excerpts because the site blocked automated fetches. The RLA-ES expansion was not run, so the number of forms it yields and whether it generates enclitics are unknown. The Leipzig word lists are ambiguous between CC BY and CC BY-NC, so they are left out.

The licence logic mirrors the English list. All of GPL, LGPL, MPL and CC BY-SA permit commercial use; they differ in what must be offered alongside. MPL is the lightest: recipients must be told where to get the source of the MPL-covered file, and the rest ships under any licence ([MPL FAQ](https://www.mozilla.org/en-US/MPL/2.0/FAQ/)). A list expanded and filtered from RLA-ES is a modified version of it, so that file would be published under MPL while the app's code stays proprietary. This follows the "mere aggregation" position the FSF gave Apache OpenOffice for dictionaries ([AOO issue 65039](https://bz.apache.org/ooo/show_bug.cgi?id=65039)), which has not been tested in court for word lists. Share-alike on CC BY-SA databases attaches when a "substantial portion" of the contents is included ([Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). The wordfreq author reads it more strictly still ([wordfreq](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/rspeer/wordfreq/master/README.md)). Whether a score computed from CC BY-SA frequencies is a derivative is contested, so the safe rule is the existing one: CC BY-SA data never touches the shipped file.

Conjugations are the scale problem. A typical Spanish verb yields around 90 simple forms ([ULPGC](https://accedacris.ulpgc.es/bitstream/10553/10233/1/0681480_00000_0000.pdf)). Lemma lists of 60,000 to 90,000 expand to 550,000 to 780,000 forms, about three quarters of them verb forms by the FreeLing counts above. No source describes how Spanish constructors or word-game makers limit them, so the controls below are inference, not established practice:

- Score every inflected form by its own Ngram frequency, so unattested forms fall below the fill floor.
- Tier by morphology: keep infinitives, participles, gerunds and third-person present and preterite near the lemma's score, and demote other persons and tenses.
- Demote hard or drop vosotros forms, voseo forms and the future subjunctive. This also keeps fill neutral between Spain and Latin America.
- Generate no enclitic combinations, and whitelist the few that read as words (DIME, DAME, VETE).
- When stripping accents merges forms (HABLO and HABLÓ), keep the highest score and carry the lemma and tag through to the clue writer.

Ngrams skew literary and mix Spain with Latin America. Subtitle lists are over 92% translated from English in the SUBTLEX-ESP case ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03240v2)), which is another reason not to lean on them.

## Nobody sells a Spanish daily as a product

Spanish digital crosswords are retention modules on rented platforms. El País relaunched its section in 2021 with an expert crossword, a mini, 365 puzzles selected by Tarkus and the digitised archive of Mambrino, who died in 2019 ([Red de Periodistas](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/)). Sources disagree on whether its supplier is Amuse Labs or Arkadium ([Laboratorio de Periodismo](https://laboratoriodeperiodismo.org/los-periodicos-retoman-las-apuestas-por-las-secciones-de-juegos-y-pasatiempos/)). The rest of the publisher picture:

- **El Debate** offers a free daily crossword and a mini ([El Debate](https://www.eldebate.com/juegos/crucigrama/)).
- **La Nación (Argentina)** runs several crossword variants free to play, with the ranking and play-history calendar reserved for subscribers, no saved progress and no dedicated games app ([La Nación FAQ](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/juegos/ln-juegos-preguntas-frecuentes-nid17062026/)).
- **La Nación (Costa Rica)** lists a Crucigrama Clásico and a Crucigrama Mini in its Premium catalogue ([nacion.com](https://www.nacion.com/suscripcion-digital-premium/)).
- **Emol (Chile)** publishes an interactive crossword that looks weekly ([Emol](https://www.emol.com/servicios/juegos/crucigrama/2026/11/05/crucigrama.html)).
- **United States:** the only publisher offering found is the Washington Post's Arkadium-supplied Spanish pages ([Washington Post](https://games.washingtonpost.com/es/juegos/crucigramas-faciles)). Searches for Univision, Telemundo, La Opinión and El Nuevo Herald returned nothing, which is not proof of absence.
- **Not checked:** El Mundo, Clarín, Infobae, El Universal and El Tiempo.

Apps are a different market: free, ad-supported, offline packs with no dated daily. The leading iOS example claims 5,489 autodefinidos and ranks 16th in Spain's free Trivia chart, and one of its reviewers reports finishing every puzzle ([App Store ES](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/id1627932335)). Google Play install figures could not be retrieved. Social features are thin: El País advertised a multiplayer mode and an invite link ([internetpasoapaso](https://internetpasoapaso.com/mejores-paginas-webs-crucigramas/)), and La Nación has its paywalled ranking. No real-time race, friends graph or open streak-and-leaderboard product was found in Spanish.

Audience signals are large but indirect:

- The Instituto Cervantes counts **635.7 million** Spanish speakers and potential users worldwide ([Euronews](https://es.euronews.com/2025/10/28/el-espanol-suma-30-millones-de-hablantes-y-alcanza-los-635-millones-en-el-mundo)).
- About **44.9 million** people in the US speak Spanish at home ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-speak-spanish-at-home/country/united-states/)).
- Spanish Wordle went from about 1,000 users to 52,000 in its first week ([Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/videojuegos/habla-daniel-rodriguez-creador-wordle-espanol-asi-adapto-dos-tardes-juego-online-moda)), with **Spain at 88% of users** in January 2022 ([Los Andes](https://www.losandes.com.ar/muy-tecno/wordle-el-juego-online-que-se-volvio-viral-y-rompio-las-reglas-de-internet)).
- El País reports 497,000 subscribers and counts games among its retention features, with no games-specific numbers ([Laboratorio de Periodismo](https://laboratoriodeperiodismo.org/el-pais-supera-los-500-000-suscriptores-tras-aumentar-un-14-su-base-de-abonados-en-el-ultimo-ano/)).
- Print puzzle sales in Spain rose more than 30% during the 2020 lockdown, according to Keesing ([Red de Periodistas](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/)).

No traffic or play counts for any Spanish publisher's games, no search-volume comparison by country and no data on US Hispanic puzzle habits were found. Willingness to pay is proven only for print and news bundles; no standalone Spanish puzzle subscription was found anywhere.

## Recommendation: adopt the letters, test the large

**Conventions to adopt.**

- **Keep the American grid.** Full crossing, rotational symmetry and a three-letter minimum are the product, and Spanish already has a name for them ("crucigrama americano"). The first-run explainer should say so in one line.
- **Take the letter rules whole.** Use a 27-letter alphabet with Ñ distinct, strip accents and diéresis in the grid, and give CH, LL and RR two squares. Accented spellings can still appear in clues and post-solve displays.
- **Label clues Horizontales and Verticales.** The app's current Spanish interface uses arrows, which is fine for the clue bar; the clue lists and marketing copy should use the words solvers know.
- **Refuse the traditional valves.** No reversed entries, masked partials, "letra repetida", chemical symbols as two-letter fill or ISO codes. They are tolerated in Spanish puzzles but are incompatible with the format and are the weakest part of the tradition.
- **Clue in the newspaper register.** Write short definitions with an occasional oblique one in the Mambrino and Tarkus manner, not long dictionary formulas. Keep the existing content rule; the ARA complaint shows double entendre draws objections.
- **Title themed puzzles by subject.** That is what "temático" means to a Spanish solver. Introduce wordplay themers with an explicit note, because the device is unfamiliar.
- **Write neutral Spanish.** Label unavoidable regionalisms the way dictionaries do.

**Word-list build.**

- **Validity:** olea/lemarios (public domain) plus RLA-ES taken under MPL, with its country variants used to tag regional words.
- **Names and titles:** Wikidata labels ranked by es.wikipedia pageviews, both CC0.
- **Commonness:** Google Books Ngrams with attribution, allowed only to lower a score, as in the English list.
- **Liveliness:** model-proposed entries and hand overrides on top, as with `lively.dict` and `rescore.dict`.
- **Private list only:** FrequencyWords, wordfreq and Wiktionary.
- **Never:** the RAE dictionary, FISE, and unlicensed GitHub lists.
- **Publication:** publish the derived list with a NOTICES file, as the English production list already is.

**Risks and open questions, in order of weight.**

1. **15×15 fill quality is unproven.** No constructor testimony exists. A fill trial on the assembled list (themed 15×15s at the current score floors, counting failures and weak entries) should come before any commitment to a Spanish large.
2. **Solver acceptance is unknown.** No evidence shows how Spanish solvers react to fully crossed grids or wordplay themes.
3. **Regional mix is a guess.** The 88% Spain figure is from one game in one month of 2022.
4. **Clue quality depends on disambiguation.** Accent-stripped homographs and conjugated forms need the lemma and tag passed to the clue writer. LLM clue quality in Spanish was not researched.
5. **Paying behaviour is untested.** Spanish solvers are used to free digital puzzles, and dollar pricing likely overshoots much of Latin America. That is an inference; no pricing data was gathered.
6. **The licence position is reasoned, not adjudicated.** Counsel should confirm the MPL-derived list, the Ngrams-derived scores and any in-house use of FreeLing.
7. **Primary puzzles were not inspected.** El País, El Debate and PasatiemposWeb grids could not be opened. An hour of manual solving would settle whether their minis are fully crossed and how they handle Ñ.

## Conclusion

The research reverses the expected difficulty. Conventions looked like the hard question and the word list a matter of download and licence. In fact the letter rules are close to uncontested, while the list has to be built from parts because the obvious authorities (RAE, FISE) are legally closed and no constructor community has published a scored alternative. That absence is also the opening: a scored, permissively licensed Spanish fill list would be an asset no competitor found here has.

The staging follows from the evidence. Mini and midi in Spanish carry little format risk, since minis already run at three publishers. The themed 15×15 and the paid tier are the two bets with no precedent, and each can be tested cheaply before it is promised: the first with an offline fill trial, the second by watching whether Spanish players use the free dailies and races at all.

What this changes:

- **Full crossing is already the norm in Spanish newspapers' digital puzzles.** All four publisher grids are fully crossed with American-style numbering, labelled "H" and "V". The assumption that Spanish solvers expect unchecked squares does not hold for these outlets.
- **Two-letter entries are the norm too, including in the product sold as "americano".** Refusing them is a real departure from what Spanish solvers see daily, and the place our puzzles would differ.
- **A fully crossed Spanish 15-wide grid without two-letter entries exists.** elro's themeless has three 15-letter phrases at 14.6% blocks. That is one constructor's hand-built grid, not proof an engine can do it daily, but it removes "nobody has done it".
- **El País's minis are built by a named constructor.** elro's Crosshare profile says they have built minis for El País since January 2026 and publishes "mainly American-style crosswords in Spanish". They are the obvious person to ask about construction, and say they want to talk about Spanish versus English construction.
- **El País meters its games.** The first puzzle played after a video ad; the second asked for registration, with "subscribe to play without limits". The Sanoja weekly, Experto, Mambrino and Tarkus puzzles could not be opened without an account, so the classic El País grid is still unverified.
- **El País lists a weekly crossword by Nataly Sanoja**, a second named constructor not in the report above.
