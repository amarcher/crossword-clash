# Spanish crossword conventions (Spain and Latin America)

Researcher's note on evidence quality. About 30 searches and page reads were made on 2026-11-13. Spanish-language sources that state construction rules explicitly turned out to be scarce: newspapers publish puzzles, not style guides, and the Spanish Wikipedia articles on *crucigrama* and *autodefinido* are thin and largely unsourced. The firmest evidence here is (a) one fully transcribed sample puzzle, (b) press articles about named setters, (c) Wikipedia statements that are themselves uncited. Each finding says which kind it is. Where a point rests only on a search-result summary and the page itself was not opened, it is marked "(search summary only)". Several things widely believed about Spanish crosswords could not be sourced in this session and are listed under Gaps rather than asserted.

## 1. Main formats and how they differ; popularity by country

### Takeaway
Spanish Wikipedia and press sources agree on a family of formats: the classic crucigrama (numbered grid, clue lists outside), the autodefinido (clues printed inside the grid, Scandinavian origin), the crucigrama blanco (solver places the black squares), the silábico (one syllable per square), plus picture/"con personaje" and themed variants. I found no reliable figures ranking their popularity by country; the country comparison is mostly a gap.

### Cited Findings
- Spanish Wikipedia defines a crucigrama as a grid ("rejilla") plus numbered "referencias" split into horizontales and verticales; across answers are written left to right and down answers top to bottom, with the stated exception that an answer may be entered backwards, flagged in the clue with "al revés". — [Wikipedia ES, Crucigrama](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucigrama)
- Variants listed there: autodefinido (clues inside the grid at the start of the word, and shorter); crucigrama blanco (no black squares shown, the solver must find where they go); silábico (a syllable per square); "con personaje" (a photo of a person, whose name is one or more answers); críptico (described as especially British, *The Times* as the reference); "translator" (for language learning). — [Wikipedia ES, Crucigrama](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucigrama)
- Autodefinido, per Spanish Wikipedia: separator squares hold the clues; one separator square may hold two clues (one across, one down); an arrow often shows the direction. Listed sub-variants: silábico, temático (a topic symbol replaces the clue for some words), gráfico (some clues are drawings), autopanal (all words of 4 or 6 letters, interlocked like a honeycomb), blanco (word lengths unknown). The article cites no sources. — [Wikipedia ES, Autodefinido](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodefinido)
- The same article illustrates the format with a Dutch "Zweeds raadsel" and files it under Commons' "Swedish-style crosswords"; English Wikipedia says the arrowword is "often called the Scandinavian crossword, as it is believed to have originated in Sweden", has no clue numbers, usually no symmetry, often a theme, and sometimes photos in place of blocks of squares. — [Wikipedia ES, Autodefinido](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodefinido); [Wikipedia EN, Crossword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)
- A Spanish solver on the Straight Dope board (poster "Nava", 3 Feb 2017) describes the Spanish range as: basic crucigramas ("one letter per square, definitions on the side"), puzzles with "one syllable in each white square", and puzzles with "the definitions written inside the black squares", which may be letter-per-square or syllable-per-square. — [Straight Dope, Crossword puzzles in other languages](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/crossword-puzzles-in-other-languages/778514?page=2)
- A 2025 essay by Francisco M. Carriscondo Esquivel (elDiario.es / Archiletras) lists the types as "sin celdas separadoras, por letras, silábicos o ilustrados" and says puzzles are graded by minutes available and difficulty. — [elDiario.es, Elogio del crucigrama](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)
- Spain, El País (digital): the 2021 relaunch offered an expert crossword, a "crucigrama mini", archives of Mambrino and Tarkus, themed word searches and sudokus, on AmuseLabs' PuzzleMe platform. — [Red de Periodistas, 6 Sep 2021](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/); [El País on X](https://x.com/el_pais/status/1413482349698994176)
- Spain, print market: David Wakefield of Keesing Media Group Spain said print puzzle sales rose more than 30% during the 2020 lockdown. — [Red de Periodistas](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/)
- Argentina: La Nación's games section carries separately branded crossword products including "Cruci experto" and "Cruci temático" alongside its standard crucigrama. — [La Nación Juegos, Cruci experto](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/juegos/cruci-experto/); [La Nación Juegos, Cruci temático](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/juegos/cruci-tematico/) (search summary only; pages not opened)
- Argentina: a La Nación column (Daniel Gigena, 28 Dec 2018) calls the grid a "grilla", recalls newsstand crossword magazines themed by sport, geography, science and the arts, notes that a photo of a celebrity sometimes replaced a written clue, and says whole collections of crossword magazines are still sold at bus terminals. — [La Nación, Crucigramas, mucho más que un pasatiempo](https://lanacion.com.ar/opinion/crucigramas-mucho-mas-pasatiempo-nid2205687)
- Apps: "Crucigrama — Autodefinido" (MBEX, App Store Spain) describes itself as "inspirado en los crucigramas escandinavos", claims 45,000+ words and 5,489 autodefinidos, and shows a 4.7 rating from about 2,500 ratings on the Spanish store. — [App Store ES](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/id1627932335)
- History that explains the naming: the first crossword in Spain appeared on 22 March 1925 in *Blanco y Negro* (pp. 52–53, section "Pasatiempos de moda") under the title "El rompecabezas de las palabras cruzadas"; the word *crucigrama* is first recorded in Spanish in 1935 (Dulce María Loynaz, per CORDE) and entered the RAE dictionary's 1947 supplement. Only this one source gives the 1925 date. — [elDiario.es, Elogio del crucigrama](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)

### Inferences
- "Palabras cruzadas" was the original Spanish name and survives as a synonym (used in the 1925 title and in a 2013 Colombian-authored column in La Opinión); "grilla" is the Argentine word for the grid. A product in Spanish will meet both vocabularies. — basis: [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html), [La Opinión](https://laopinion.com/2013/08/24/un-juego-de-palabras-util/), [La Nación](https://lanacion.com.ar/opinion/crucigramas-mucho-mas-pasatiempo-nid2205687)
- The autodefinido has a real standalone app audience in Spain (2,500 ratings for one app), so it is a mainstream format there and not a curiosity.
- Photo/"personaje" clues are attested independently in Spain (Wikipedia) and Argentina (Gigena), so they look like a shared magazine convention.

### Gaps
- No source found that ranks formats by popularity in Spain, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Chile or among US Spanish speakers. Nothing usable was found on Mexico (El Universal), Colombia (El Tiempo), Chile, or US Spanish-language papers; the one La Opinión (Los Angeles) piece found is a humorous column with no format detail.
- Nothing found on Clarín's puzzles or setters ("Claringrilla" returned no usable results), nor on Argentine puzzle magazine publishers by name.
- When the autodefinido reached Spain and Latin America, and through which publishers, is not covered by any source read.
- Spanish puzzle-magazine publishers' own "cómo se juega" pages were not located.

## 2. Grid rules in the classic format

### Takeaway
No Spanish-language source read states grid rules (size, black-square share, symmetry, crossing, minimum length) as a norm. The concrete evidence is one sample: a 2025 puzzle from an independent Spanish-language site whose clue list includes many two-letter entries, and a third-party description of a Mambrino grid as 12 × 13. Treat everything else as unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- A Mambrino puzzle for El País dated 25 April 2016 is described by its uploader as a 12-row by 13-column grid. This is a Scribd description, not El País itself, and the grid was not viewable. — [Scribd, Crucigrama Mambrino 25 abril 2016](https://es.scribd.com/document/729494935/304)
- Symmetry: a Spanish solver wrote in 2017 that "In Spanish they may have symmetry; today's grids in [El País] have a level two rotation", that symmetry is "more likely" for basic letter-per-square crucigramas and less likely for syllabic ones. One person's observation. — [Straight Dope](https://boards.straightdope.com/t/crossword-puzzles-in-other-languages/778514?page=2)
- An Argentine construction helper tool ("Asistente crucigrámico", itch.io) works on a 15×15 grid and checks black squares against "la simetría tradicional de los crucigramas". — [itch.io, Asistente crucigrámico](https://davidtmarchand.itch.io/asistente-crucigramico) (search summary only)
- Two-letter entries, sample evidence: in the acernuda.com puzzle "Serpiente de Esparto" (author Alejandro Cernuda, created 12/11/2025, 72 numbered clues, numbered per word with separate HORIZONTALES and VERTICALES lists), a large share of clues can only have two- or three-letter answers: "Distrito Federal", "Las dos primeras letras del abecedario", "Símbolo químico: Tántalo", "Símbolo químico: Rutenio", "Símbolo químico: Aluminio", "Dios egipcio del sol", "Código ISO2 para el país Angola", "Código ISO2 para el país Emiratos Árabes Unidos", "Letra repetida" (three times), "Nombre de letra" (three times), "Iniciales de escritor francés autor de Los tres mosqueteros". — [acernuda.com, Serpiente de Esparto (PDF)](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)
- For comparison, the neighbouring Romance traditions as described by English Wikipedia (uncited there): Italian grids are usually oblong (13×21 common), usually not symmetrical, allow two-letter words and minimise black squares; French grids are 8–13 a side, need no symmetry, allow two-letter words, and "a black-square usage of 10% is typical". The article gives no equivalent description for Spanish. — [Wikipedia EN, Crossword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)
- Mexican school material (SEP, Nueva Escuela Mexicana) on designing a crossword says black squares separate words, that words should be distributed as evenly as possible, and that numbering may run continuously or restart at 1 for verticals and horizontals. This is classroom guidance, not newspaper practice. — [SEP, ¿Cómo diseñar un crucigrama?](https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/coleccion/19049/) (search summary only)

### Inferences
- Two-letter entries are accepted without apology in at least some current Spanish-language puzzles: the sample fills them with initials, symbols, country codes and "letra repetida". This matches the French and Italian traditions next door, which explicitly allow them.
- The sample is numbered per word, American fashion, with one clue per number. Whether El País or La Vanguardia number per word or per row/column was not confirmed (see Gaps).
- Rotational symmetry appears to be common but optional, on the strength of one solver comment and one Argentine tool; it should not be described as a rule.

### Gaps
- Proportion of black squares in Spanish newspaper grids: no source found.
- Whether every letter must be crossed: no source found that states it either way. My background understanding is that Spanish newspaper grids are largely fully crossed (closer to the French/Italian model than to the British lattice), and that the "many black squares and unchecked letters" picture fits some magazine and educational puzzles more than El País; I could not source this, so it must be checked against actual grids.
- Minimum entry length as a stated rule: none found. One-letter "entries" (isolated squares clued as "Vocal", "Consonante") are something I recall from Spanish newspaper puzzles but could not confirm.
- Per-row numbering with several answers in one line separated by full stops (the layout I recall from El País and La Vanguardia) could not be confirmed; the Scribd transcriptions that would show it were not readable.
- Typical sizes beyond the single 12×13 report: none confirmed.
- The acernuda.com sample looks software-assisted and is one site's habit; it is evidence that such fill is tolerated, not that leading setters use it at the same rate.

## 3. Letter handling: Ñ, accents, digraphs, abbreviations, symbols, reversals, partials

### Takeaway
Ñ is its own letter and accents are dropped; CH and LL now take two squares, and were reportedly one square in older puzzles, though the source for that is an uncited Wikipedia sentence. Abbreviations, initials, chemical symbols, ISO codes, letter names, reversed entries and partial words are all attested as fill in a current sample.

### Cited Findings
- English Wikipedia: accent marks are ignored in crosswords "except the tilde in Spanish"; "in Spanish, N and Ñ are distinct letters"; "the digraphs *ch* and *ll* fill two squares", whereas in older puzzles (it says before the 1996 spelling reform) they occupied one square. The passage has no citation and the section is tagged as needing citations. — [Wikipedia EN, Crossword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)
- The RAE states that ch and ll were counted as letters of the Spanish alphabet from the 1754 orthography until they were definitively excluded in 2010 as digraphs; the alphabet now has 27 letters, including ñ. This conflicts with Wikipedia's "1996" date for the change (the RAE page gives 2010 for the exclusion). — [RAE, Exclusión de ch y ll del abecedario](https://www.rae.es/consultas/exclusion-de-ch-y-ll-del-abecedario) (search summary only)
- Mexican school guidance: accents are omitted because a crossing might put an accented vowel where the other word has none; CH, RR and LL count as two characters, i.e. two squares. — [SEP, Nueva Escuela Mexicana](https://nuevaescuelamexicana.sep.gob.mx/contenido/coleccion/19242/) (search summary only)
- Ñ in software: the Fortuny crossword app's version history records a fix for crosswords containing "ñ", which shows Ñ is entered as a distinct letter in his grids. — [App Store, Crucigramas Jordi Fortuny](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1016442690)
- Reversal is a recognised device: Spanish Wikipedia says an answer that does not fit may be entered in the opposite direction, signalled with "al revés". — [Wikipedia ES, Crucigrama](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucigrama)
- Sample puzzle, exact wording of fill devices (acernuda.com, 2025):
  - Reversal: "Invertido - Unidad de cuidados intensivos." (i.e. UCI entered backwards).
  - Chemical symbols: "Símbolo químico: Tántalo", "Símbolo químico: Rutenio", "Símbolo químico: Aluminio".
  - Initialisms and abbreviations: "Distrito Federal.", "Colegio Rural Agrupado.", "Euskadiko Ezquerra (Izquierda de Euskadi) En España.", "Esquerra Republicana de Catalunya (Izquierda Republicana de Cataluña) En España.", "Abreviatura de moneda de China Yuan/Renminbi."
  - Codes: "Código ISO2 para el país Angola", "Código ISO3 para el país Níger".
  - Initials of people: "Iniciales de escritor francés autor de Los tres mosqueteros.", "Iniciales de escritora y dramaturga británica. Autora de Los diez negritos."
  - Letters as fill: "Letra repetida.", "Nombre de letra.", "Las dos primeras letras del abecedario."
  - Partial words, with the site's own notation: "(***IMADO) - Persona que vive en casa ajena…", "(**EMPIES) - Miriópodo…", "(SOM*T*LOG*A) - Tratado de las partes sólidas del cuerpo humano." The site's legend: "(*) sustituye alguna letra de la palabra. Ej.: (***A) Primeras letras de Morada, vivienda = CAS".
  - Multi-word answers run together without spaces: legend example "Frase (...) y (...) Novela de Tolstoi = GUERRAPAZ".
  - Foreign words: "La conjunción "o" traducida al inglés."
  - Repetition clue: "Repetido es arrullo para dormir a los bebés."
  - Generic clues: "Ciudad de España.", "Nombre de país.", "Nombre propio de hombre.", "Forma verbal. Escuché."
  — [acernuda.com, Serpiente de Esparto (PDF)](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)
- Convention on abbreviations in clues, from a La Nación (Argentina, via GDA) tips piece of 16 Dec 2025: if the clue is abbreviated the answer is too; the clue's verb tense matches the answer's. The article was AI-assisted and reads as adapted from English-language advice, so it is weak evidence of Spanish practice. — [La Nación / nacion.com](https://www.nacion.com/ciencia/el-truco-mas-usado-para-resolver-crucigramas-y/OLTQES5ZPNFTTMDPCFVFDPGPVQ/story/)

### Inferences
- Safe defaults for a Spanish grid: 27-letter alphabet with Ñ distinct; strip á é í ó ú and ü to the base vowel in the grid; CH, LL and RR as two squares each. All three points have at least one source and no source contradicts them for current practice.
- The one-square CH/LL convention, if it existed, would have followed the pre-reform alphabet in which they were letters; it is historical only.
- Partial words in Spanish practice are clued as "the first/last letters of X" or with a masked-letter pattern, which is a different idea from the American fill-in-the-blank partial.

### Gaps
- No primary source (a puzzle or setter) confirms that CH/LL ever shared one square in Spanish crosswords; the claim rests on uncited Wikipedia text with a date that does not match the RAE's.
- No source found on Roman numerals as fill in Spanish puzzles (none appear in the sample), nor on musical notes, cardinal points or "consonante/vocal" single letters, all of which I recall as common but could not confirm.
- Whether ü is treated as u was not stated by any source read; it is assumed with the other diacritics.
- The exact tags used by the big newspaper setters ("(Al revés)", "(Pl.)", "Símbolo de…") were not seen in a primary puzzle; the sample uses "Invertido -" and "Símbolo químico:". The Wikipedia wording is "al revés".

## 4. Clue style, register and named setters

### Takeaway
Two registers coexist: plain dictionary-definition cluing (the magazine and generator norm, often long and lifted from the DLE) and short witty "author's definitions" associated with newspaper setters such as Mambrino, Tarkus and Fortuny. Wordplay in the Spanish newspaper tradition is a pun or oblique definition, not a British-style cryptic.

### Cited Findings
- Clues are technically called "referencias"; good crosswords are "pequeños diccionarios de autor" in which the setter's wit escapes the "férreas cadenas definicionales" of dictionaries, often through figurative senses and wordplay. — [elDiario.es, Elogio del crucigrama](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)
- Verbatim examples from that essay: Tarkus, "lugar donde se hacen castillos" = AIRE and "el final de un apasionado beso" = AIRE; Mambrino, "lo que tiembla al llegar el postre" = FLAN and "ejercitar la pituitaria" = OLER; Olissip, "una cordillera que corta la madera" = SIERRA and "una casa de tortolitos" = NIDO. — [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)
- Setters work under pseudonyms ("se ocultan bajo heterónimos"): Mambrino, Tarkus, Olissip, Litero. Reference works for solvers and setters: Fausto Turell, *Diccionario auxiliar del crucigramista* (1978); María Socorro Pérez Quejero, *Diccionario para crucigramas* (1996). Olissip's crossword books are published by Alma. — [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)
- Mambrino was El País's "crucigramista histórico", died in 2019, and his puzzles were digitised for the 2021 relaunch; Tarkus chose his best 365 puzzles for the archive. Neither real name is given. — [Red de Periodistas](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/)
- Relative difficulty, one reader's view (Evaristo Fuentes Melián, 16 Jul 2019): Tarkus's new daily puzzles in El País "son harto difíciles… los doy por imposibles a las 3 am", whereas the late Mambrino's gave "dulce somnolencia"; "Tarkus, apiádate de nosotros." — [Diario del Valle blog](http://diariodelvalledelaorotava.blogspot.com/2019/07/los-crucigramas-de-tarkus.html)
- Jordi Fortuny (La Vanguardia): per a Màrius Serra column of 6 April 2026, read through an aggregator copy, he had set the paper's crossword for almost 39 years before a medical leave; Jep Ferret substituted, and the two now alternate weekly. — [aggregator copy of La Vanguardia, "Bienvenido, Jordi Fortuny"](https://newsar.codejungle.org/articles/54162) (search summary only)
- Fortuny's own app describes his style as "misterioso, clásico, incluso picante a veces" and says he had published in La Vanguardia for almost 30 years (older copy). — [App Store, Crucigramas Jordi Fortuny](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1016442690)
- Morphological hints are part of the solving culture: an infinitive ends in -r, an imperfect contains -ba-/-ía-; a Colombian-authored column likewise mentions clues that give the ending of an infinitive or participle or the sound of a vowel or consonant, and solvers reaching for the Larousse for "palabras insólitas". — [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html); [La Opinión, 24 Aug 2013](https://laopinion.com/2013/08/24/un-juego-de-palabras-util/)
- Dictionary register in the sample puzzle: clues are full DLE-style definitions, some very long, with lexicographic contour phrases such as "Dicho de una sustancia, de un dispositivo, de un procedimiento, etc.: Que desatasca o sirve para desatascar." and "Dicho del aire: Dar en algo, refrescándolo." Short ones also occur: "Alabar.", "Incitar, estimular.", "Trampa, emboscada.", "Ausencia de ley." — [acernuda.com sample](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)
- Regional marking follows dictionary practice: the sample clues a regionalism as "Bol. hierba mora." (the DLE's Bolivia label) and uses gentilics from across the Spanish-speaking world: "Natural de Treinta y Tres, ciudad o departamento de Uruguay.", "Natural de Ávila, ciudad o provincia de España." — [acernuda.com sample](https://crucigrama.acernuda.com/2025-serpiente-de-esparto/pdf)
- The app Dicciogramas states that its definitions come from the VOX *Diccionario general de la lengua española*. — [App Store, Dicciogramas](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1429928855) (search summary only)
- Risqué double meanings are part of the newspaper tradition and now draw complaints. In the Catalan-language daily ARA (not a Spanish-language puzzle, but the same Barcelona press culture as Fortuny), a reader complained in March 2026 that Jordi Ventura's clues were sexist; the ombudsman (Antoni Batista, 25 Apr 2026) held that wordplay and double meaning are central to crosswords and that entertainment has more licence than news, while asking for sensitivity. — [ARA, Crucigramas, sexo y sensibilidad](https://es.ara.cat/defensor-del-lector/crucigramas-sexo-sensibilidad_129_5718923.html)

### Inferences
- The prestige style in Spain is a short, sly definition with a figurative reading (FLAN, AIRE, NIDO); a question mark is not needed to signal it. This is closer to the playful end of American cluing than to cryptics, so it is adaptable to a fully crossed grid.
- The mass-market style is the dictionary definition, including the DLE's "Dicho de…" formula and country labels. Solvers used to that register will find it normal for clues to be longer than English ones.
- Regional vocabulary is handled by labelling (country abbreviation or "En España") rather than by avoiding it, at least where definitions derive from the DLE.
- Setter identity matters in Spain: puzzles are sold and remembered by pseudonym (archives "de Mambrino y Tarkus", a Fortuny app), which is unlike the mostly anonymous magazine market.

### Gaps
- No interview with Mambrino, Tarkus or Fortuny about method was found, so nothing first-hand on how they build grids or what they forbid themselves.
- No primary Mambrino, Tarkus or Fortuny puzzle was readable in this session (El País and La Vanguardia games pages were not opened; Scribd transcriptions returned no clue text), so their use of "(Al revés)", "(Pl.)", "Símbolo de…", clue length and two-letter fill is unverified.
- Clarín's setters and style: nothing found.
- No systematic source on Spain versus Latin America vocabulary differences in clues (vosotros forms, ordenador/computadora and the like); only the dictionary-label practice above.
- Typical clue length was not measured; only the qualitative contrast between the two registers is supported.

## 5. Are themed puzzles a convention?

### Takeaway
Themes exist, but in a different form from the American themed 15×15: the attested forms are topic-based puzzles and magazines (sport, geography, arts), a themed product line at La Nación, themed autodefinidos where a topic symbol replaces clues, and puzzles built around a pictured personality. I found no evidence of American-style long theme entries with a wordplay gimmick as a Spanish convention.

### Cited Findings
- "Con personaje" variant: a photo of a person, with one or more answers being their name or surname. — [Wikipedia ES, Crucigrama](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucigrama)
- Autodefinido "temático": a generic topic symbol replaces the clue for some words; "gráfico": some clues are drawings. — [Wikipedia ES, Autodefinido](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodefinido)
- Argentine magazines were organised by subject (sport, geography, science, arts, with more show-business figures than writers or painters), and a celebrity photo could stand in for a clue. — [La Nación, Gigena 2018](https://lanacion.com.ar/opinion/crucigramas-mucho-mas-pasatiempo-nid2205687)
- La Nación offers a product named "Cruci temático", and its own solving guide uses the example "si el crucigrama tiene un tema de 'películas'". — [La Nación Juegos, Cruci temático](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/juegos/cruci-tematico/) (search summary only); [La Nación, Cómo resolver un crucigrama](https://lanacion.com.ar/juegos/trucos-y-consejos/como-resolver-un-crucigrama-nivel-facil-intermedio-y-dificil-nid10072024)
- El País's relaunch includes a themed word search updated every Sunday by Marisa Sanjurjo; the article mentions no themed crossword. — [Red de Periodistas](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/)
- Swedish-style puzzles in general "often have a theme such as literature or geography". — [Wikipedia EN, Crossword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossword)

### Inferences
- "Temático" in Spanish means the answers (or many of them) belong to a subject area. A Spanish solver would understand a puzzle titled by topic at once; the American device of hidden wordplay linking a few long entries would be new to most and would need explaining.
- The pictured-personality puzzle is the closest thing to a signature themed format in the Spanish magazine tradition.

### Gaps
- No source confirms or rules out wordplay-themed crosswords by Spanish-language setters, including newer independent constructors.
- La Nación's "Cruci temático" page was not opened, so how its theme works is unverified.

## 6. What solvers say they like and dislike

### Takeaway
Direct solver testimony was hard to find. What exists praises wit in the definitions, treats difficulty as a matter of setter (Tarkus hard, Mambrino soothing), and accepts odd dictionary words as part of the game; I found no forum or review thread complaining specifically about two-letter fill or crosswordese.

### Cited Findings
- Praise goes to the wit of the definition: the elDiario.es essayist singles out punning clues as what makes a crossword an "author's dictionary" and recommends solving without outside help. — [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html)
- Excess difficulty is a stated dislike: a long-time El País solver calls Tarkus's dailies "harto difíciles" and begs "apiádate de nosotros", preferring Mambrino. — [Diario del Valle blog, 2019](http://diariodelvalledelaorotava.blogspot.com/2019/07/los-crucigramas-de-tarkus.html)
- Obscure words are seen as characteristic, with affection: a columnist describes a solver who went to the Larousse for "palabras insólitas" and calls a favourite setter a "crucigramista de cabecera". — [La Opinión, 2013](https://laopinion.com/2013/08/24/un-juego-de-palabras-util/)
- Crosswordese is acknowledged but not itemised: the La Nación/GDA tips piece says recurring answers exist and advises doing short words first and memorising common abbreviations (historical events, military ranks, forms of address). — [nacion.com, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.nacion.com/ciencia/el-truco-mas-usado-para-resolver-crucigramas-y/OLTQES5ZPNFTTMDPCFVFDPGPVQ/story/)
- Taste and tone complaints exist: the ARA reader said sexual double-entendre clues were sexist and in poor taste, and also complained of spelling errors; the setter admitted the errors. — [ARA, 25 Apr 2026](https://es.ara.cat/defensor-del-lector/crucigramas-sexo-sensibilidad_129_5718923.html)
- Audience: the essayist says retirees are reputed to be the biggest consumers; Gigena describes group solving in a care home. — [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/cultura/elogio-crucigrama_1_12194868.html); [La Nación](https://lanacion.com.ar/opinion/crucigramas-mucho-mas-pasatiempo-nid2205687)
- App reviews seen were brief and positive ("Si te gustan los autodefinidos, esta es tu app"; "Me he acabado todos los crucis"), with nothing on fill quality. Apps compete on volume and on not repeating content. — [App Store ES, Crucigrama — Autodefinido](https://apps.apple.com/es/app/id1627932335)
- El País subscribers asked for a puzzles app at the 2021 relaunch. — [Red de Periodistas](https://www.reddeperiodistas.com/la-web-de-el-pais-relanza-su-seccion-de-crucigramas-y-juegos/)

### Inferences
- The absence of complaints about two-letter fill, alongside its open use in a current puzzle, suggests solvers treat symbols, initials and "letra repetida" as part of the form. This is an argument from silence and is weak.
- Finishing every puzzle in an app ("me he acabado todos") and the volume claims in store listings point to appetite for quantity and a steady daily supply.

### Gaps
- No Spanish-language forum, Reddit or review thread was found that discusses crosswordese, two-letter fill or obscure words directly; low-star app reviews were not reachable through the store pages.
- No survey or readership data on solver preferences in any Spanish-speaking country.
- No evidence gathered on how US Spanish speakers or Latin American solvers react to American-style fully crossed grids in Spanish (for example the Spanish minis some outlets publish).
